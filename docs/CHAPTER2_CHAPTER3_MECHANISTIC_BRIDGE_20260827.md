# Chapter 2 → Chapter 3 mechanistic bridge

Updated: 2026-09-06

## Purpose

Chapter 3 is not only a catalogue of which *Campanula microdonta* traits differ among Izu islands. It is the **direct phenotypic-realization layer** that follows a Chapter 2 programme which first defines conditional response geometry, then shows that world evidence saturates before the historical transition contract closes, and finally uses Izu to resolve the contemporary functional half of that chain.

The bridge is intentionally asymmetric:

```text
world island confrontation
    -> geography-first saturation + transition-measurement bottleneck
        -> Izu selected by measurement continuity and falsification value
            -> contemporary functional realization resolved in Chapter 2
                -> Chapter 3 directly measured within-lineage phenotype
```

The focal move is therefore not `world → nearby case study`. Chapter 2 expands descriptive breadth to 42 research entries across 37 exact geographic labels, preserves a separate frozen formal identifiability audit, and stops large-island expansion under a predeclared zero-novelty rule. Izu is used only after that step because the same source-linked island series connects historical reproductive evidence, repeated contemporary networks, quantitative pollinator functional traits, model-facing failures as well as successes, and the directly measured phenotype in this repository.

## Chapter 2 result that Chapter 3 inherits

The defensible Chapter 2 mechanism is not a universal island syndrome. Within the declared synthetic model, response sign depends on partner loss/arrival regime, starting functional position evaluated against the realized community, state-by-community non-additivity and local filtering; autonomous assurance modifies downstream magnitude in the tested envelope.

The world confrontation then supplies a different result: empirical response states are diverse, but the literature remains outcome-rich and historically transition-poor. Geography-first expansion does not remove that bottleneck. Small islands recover some dated transition history, but no full matched source-state → transition → realization → plant-response contract closes.

Izu resolves the next layer without pretending to solve the historical one. The historical signed-position projection organizes raw matching but does not explain null-corrected matching, and the Oshima-source sensitivity is unsupported. In contrast, contemporary pollinator functional diversity is positively associated with corrected trait matching, while matching-to-pollen propagation is weaker and plant responses branch. Chapter 2 therefore closes with **mechanism discrimination plus a localized measurement contract**, not with a claim that pollinator change historically caused the Chapter 3 phenotype.

## Trait-axis bridge

The Chapter 3 traits are classified by what part of the Chapter 2 architecture they could in principle inform. This is a measurement correspondence, not a causal assignment.

| Chapter 3 trait axis | Chapter 2 connection | Bridge status | Prohibited promotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| corolla length | closest retained morphology candidate to a plant-side mechanical matching coordinate | candidate correspondence; not calibrated to the exact source-native tube-length variable | do not substitute flattened corolla length for the exact signed tube-length coordinate |
| throat width / mouth width | floral access geometry and potential mechanical accessibility | parallel access channel | narrower width does not by itself imply greater generalization or a particular pollinator guild |
| reproductive-organ length | reproductive interface and possible contact geometry | downstream interface channel | do not infer pollen deposition or visitor effectiveness from organ length alone |
| nectar-guide coverage | visual guidance / attraction investment | parallel attraction channel | guide reduction does not prove lower pollinator dependence or a mechanical matching mechanism |
| corolla area / width | floral display and investment | parallel display channel | size divergence is not a direct estimate of effective service |
| aspect ratio / tube-flare ratio / organ-corolla ratio | proportional architecture | weak-divergence comparison channel | weak divergence does not imply mechanistic irrelevance |
| guide spatial concentration | within-flower signal allocation | weak-divergence visual channel | do not equate spatial concentration with pollinator attraction without behavioural data |

The strongest current Chapter 3 divergence is distributed across display size, access geometry, reproductive-interface length and visual-guide investment. The size/allometry gate further shows that much of this divergence is coordinated, while selected access, reproductive-interface and guide channels retain departures beyond common scaling.

## What Chapter 3 tests now

Chapter 3 directly tests:

1. whether one island lineage shows strong among-island floral differentiation;
2. how much divergence follows a shared size/investment trajectory;
3. which trait channels retain island-specific structure beyond common allometric scaling;
4. which proportional or spatial axes remain comparatively weak.

The current answer is not `one uniform syndrome` and not `every trait branches independently`. The phenotype contains a large coordinated component plus selected residual departures.

## What Chapter 3 does not establish

Chapter 3 does not identify:

- historical Bombus loss as the cause of any trait change;
- a plant-specific pollinator functional centre for each measured individual;
- single-visit pollen deposition or per-visitor effectiveness for the measured phenotype;
- reproductive dependency or mature-seed consequences linked to each measured individual;
- Qst or direct evidence of selection;
- the Chapter 2 synthetic sign-switch locations as biological thresholds.

A stronger end-to-end causal bridge remains a **prospective extension**:

```text
visitor identity + exact pollinator functional trait
    -> plant-specific visitor weights
        -> legitimate floral contact / realized matching
            -> single-visit pollen deposition / effective service
                -> controlled reproductive dependency
                    -> mature reproductive outcome
                        -> relation to the measured phenotype
```

Historical identification additionally requires a true temporal partner-regime transition with matched pre/post plant response or an independently replicated bridge-state exposure. The present phenotype can be tested against such a chain later, but it must not be back-labelled as proof of it. These missing measurements do **not** make Chapter 2 unfinished and do **not** invalidate the present Chapter 3 phenotype result.

## Dissertation role

The three chapters form a breadth-to-depth sequence rather than three exchangeable replications:

- **Chapter 1 — WHEN / WHERE:** establish the biogeographic contexts in which island-associated floral/reproductive filtering is detectable and where regional response vectors differ.
- **Chapter 2 — HOW / proximal WHY + IDENTIFIABILITY:** define conditional response geometry, establish world response diversity, show where geography-first evidence saturates, localize the historical-transition bottleneck and justify Izu by measurement continuity.
- **Chapter 3 — PHENOTYPIC REALIZATION:** determine how the focal *C. microdonta* phenotype is structured across islands as shared scaling plus selected residual departures.

The Chapter 3 contribution is therefore:

> **to show how directly measured floral divergence is organized within the focal continuity system—what moves together, and what remains island-specific after common scaling is removed.**

This remains a phenotype result. Historical mechanistic attribution is a separate prospective test, not a condition for accepting the present chapter.

# Chapter 2 → Chapter 3 mechanistic bridge

Updated: 2026-08-27

## Purpose

Chapter 3 is not only a catalogue of which *Campanula microdonta* traits differ among Izu islands. It is the focal phenotypic realization layer that follows the Chapter 2 result that a common interaction perturbation can produce conditional, community-contingent response branches.

The bridge is intentionally asymmetric:

```text
broad island comparison universe
    -> Chapter 2 response-state and mechanism boundaries
        -> Izu as the deepest empirical mechanism anchor
            -> Chapter 3 within-lineage phenotype
```

The broad comparison universe is larger than the 13 strict Chapter 2 manuscript challenges. The 13 are the source-locked external challenge set; additional source-gated and reanalysed systems were used for model development, falsification and cross-archipelago expansion. Chapter 3 does not repeat those comparisons. It uses the focal system that can be followed most deeply.

## Chapter 2 result that Chapter 3 inherits

The defensible Chapter 2 mechanism is not a universal island syndrome. Within the declared model, response sign depends on the balance of partner loss and arrival, starting functional position, realised pollinator community and local interaction filtering. Starting position organizes the mean response geometry, but community realization dominates cell-level variation and the state-by-community component is non-additive.

The empirical Izu route adds a stronger bridge than qualitative state matching alone: source-native plant functional position and the observed shift in pollinator functional centre can be compared with observed changes in plant trait matching. This is a mechanistic triangulation, not proof that pollinator change historically caused the Chapter 3 phenotype.

## Trait-axis bridge

The Chapter 3 traits are therefore classified by what part of the Chapter 2 architecture they could in principle inform. This is a measurement correspondence, not a causal assignment.

| Chapter 3 trait axis | Chapter 2 connection | Bridge status | Prohibited promotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| corolla length | closest retained morphology candidate to a plant-side mechanical matching coordinate | candidate correspondence; not calibrated to the exact source-native tube-length variable | do not substitute flattened corolla length for the exact signed tube-length coordinate |
| throat width / mouth width | floral access geometry and potential mechanical accessibility | parallel access channel | narrower width does not by itself imply greater generalization or a particular pollinator guild |
| reproductive-organ length | reproductive interface and possible contact geometry | downstream interface channel | do not infer pollen deposition or visitor effectiveness from organ length alone |
| nectar-guide coverage | visual guidance / attraction investment | parallel attraction channel | guide reduction does not prove lower pollinator dependence or a mechanical matching mechanism |
| corolla area / width | floral display and investment | parallel display channel | size divergence is not a direct estimate of effective service |
| aspect ratio / tube-flare ratio / organ-corolla ratio | proportional architecture | weak-divergence comparison channel | weak divergence does not imply mechanistic irrelevance |
| guide spatial concentration | within-flower signal allocation | weak-divergence visual channel | do not equate spatial concentration with pollinator attraction without behavioural data |

The strongest current Chapter 3 divergence is distributed across display size, access geometry, reproductive-interface length and visual-guide investment. That multidimensional and unequal response is the phenotype pattern that the Chapter 2 branching framework must be able to accommodate.

## What Chapter 3 can test now

Chapter 3 directly tests:

1. whether a single island lineage shows strong among-island floral differentiation;
2. whether divergence is multidimensional rather than captured by one island-syndrome score;
3. which trait channels carry the largest phenotypic differentiation;
4. whether absolute/investment axes and proportional-shape axes diverge equally or unequally.

The current answer is that divergence is strong but unequal: reproductive-organ length, mouth width, corolla area/length/width, guide coverage and throat width carry substantially stronger signals than several proportional or spatial-allocation metrics.

## What Chapter 3 does not test yet

Chapter 3 does not currently identify:

- historical Bombus loss as the cause of any trait change;
- a plant-specific pollinator functional centre for each measured individual;
- single-visit pollen deposition or per-visitor effectiveness;
- reproductive dependency or mature-seed consequences for the measured phenotype;
- Qst or direct evidence of selection;
- the Chapter 2 synthetic sign-switch locations as biological thresholds.

Those missing links define the prospective empirical chain:

```text
visitor identity + exact pollinator functional trait
    -> plant-specific visitor weights
        -> frozen signed plant position
            -> single-visit pollen deposition / effective service
                -> controlled reproductive dependency
                    -> mature reproductive outcome
```

Chapter 3 phenotype can then be tested as the terminal or historical realization of that chain, but the present morphological data must not be back-labelled as proof of it.

## Dissertation role

The three chapters form a zoom rather than three exchangeable replications:

- **Chapter 1 — WHEN / WHERE:** establish the biogeographic contexts in which island-associated floral/reproductive filtering is detectable and where regional response vectors differ.
- **Chapter 2 — HOW / proximal WHY:** explain why a broad interaction perturbation can produce conditional response geometry; use a broad external comparison universe for state/falsification boundaries and Izu for the deepest empirical mechanistic connection.
- **Chapter 3 — PHENOTYPIC REALIZATION:** determine which floral axes actually carry divergence within one focal Izu lineage and whether the realized phenotype is uniform or trait-specific.

The Chapter 3 contribution is therefore stronger than `what phenotype?` alone:

> **How is a condition-dependent island response architecture realized as unequal, multidimensional floral divergence within one focal lineage?**

This remains a phenotype question. Mechanistic attribution requires the direct pollination and reproductive measurements listed above.
